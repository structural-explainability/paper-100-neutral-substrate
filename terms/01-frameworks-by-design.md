# Foundational Frameworks Organized by Design Objective

Existing ontology frameworks primarily impose constraints on ontological categories, logical consistency, or conceptual taxonomies.

This work introduces structural constraints on representation substrates designed to support interoperable representations under persistent interpretive disagreement.

## Existing (what categories must exist)

world / ontology / representation

## Additional (what commitments must be excluded)

world / multiple ontologies (disagree) / neutral substrate 

## Types

Universal ontology: BFO, DOLCE, SUMO

Conceptual modeling: OntoClean, UFO

Knowledge representation: RDF / OWL

Institutional ontology: social ontology frameworks

Neutral substrate framework: interoperability under persistent disagreement

## Universal Ontology Frameworks

### Basic Formal Ontology (BFO)

Design objective: provide a shared upper ontology for scientific domains.
BFO defines high-level categories (e.g., continuant, occurrent)
intended to support consistent integration of scientific ontologies.

### DOLCE (Descriptive Ontology for Linguistic and Cognitive Engineering)

Design objective: capture categories implicit in human cognition and language.
DOLCE focuses on conceptual distinctions reflected in natural language and human conceptualization.

### SUMO (Suggested Upper Merged Ontology)

Design objective: create a comprehensive upper ontology supporting reasoning and knowledge integration.
SUMO attempts to provide a broad taxonomy suitable for automated reasoning systems.

## Representation and Knowledge Frameworks

### Cyc Ontology

Design objective: represent common-sense knowledge needed for general reasoning.
The Cyc ontology includes large numbers of concepts designed to support inference in AI systems.

### RDF / OWL Semantic Web Stack

Design objective: enable interoperable knowledge representation on the web.
These frameworks provide formal languages for expressing ontologies and linking structured data across systems.

## Foundational Modeling Methodologies

Frameworks are not ontologies they are methods for constructing them.

### OntoClean

Design objective: ensure taxonomic correctness and conceptual clarity in ontology design.
OntoClean evaluates class hierarchies using meta-properties such as rigidity and identity.

### UFO (Unified Foundational Ontology)

Design objective: provide a philosophically grounded ontology for conceptual modeling.
UFO integrates metaphysical insights into modeling languages used in information systems.

## Institutional and Normative Ontology Frameworks

### Institutional Ontology (Searle / Ferraris traditions)

Design objective: represent social and institutional entities such as obligations, roles, and rules.
These frameworks model entities that exist because of normative or institutional structures.
