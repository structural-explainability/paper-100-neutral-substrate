# Interpretive Frameworks in Ontology

Interpretive frameworks are systems of concepts, rules, and assumptions used
to assign meaning to representations.

Different frameworks may interpret the same representations differently
depending on their ontological commitments, conceptual structures, or
normative assumptions.

Interpretive frameworks typically define:

- the categories used to classify entities
- the relations recognized between entities
- the rules governing interpretation
- the conclusions derived from those interpretations

Multiple interpretive frameworks may operate over the same representation
substrate.

## Conceptual Layers in Neutral Substrates

- logic: proposition
- substrate architecture: constraints on commitments
- ontology: commitment
- framework reasoning: conclusion

## Conceptual Roles in Neutral Substrates

- proposition  → logical content
- constraint   → substrate restriction
- commitment   → ontology assertion
- conclusion   → framework inference

## In Neutral Substrates

1. frameworks may disagree about conclusions
2. ontologies asserting those conclusions create commitments
3. the substrate constrains which commitments may appear at the foundational layer

## Key Ideas

A proposition may appear as a conclusion within a framework,
but becomes an ontological commitment only when asserted
by the ontology itself.

Neutral substrates therefore constrain which propositions
may become ontological commitments.

## Example Terms

Ontological Commitment.
A proposition asserted as true within an ontology or representation system.
When an ontology asserts a proposition as part of its structure,
the proposition becomes one of the ontology's commitments.

Conclusion.
A proposition derived within an interpretive framework according to
its reasoning rules, models, or normative principles.
Different admissible frameworks may derive incompatible conclusions
concerning the same entities or events represented in the substrate.
