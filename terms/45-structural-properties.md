# Structural Properties and Vocabulary for Neutral Substrate Arguments

These terms describe how the system is built.

Substrate.
A representational or structural layer that supports multiple interpretive frameworks without embedding their commitments.

Neutral Substrate.
A substrate whose structure does not presuppose specific ontological, causal, or normative interpretations.

Interpretive Framework.
A system of concepts and rules used to assign meaning to representations within a substrate.

Extension.
An augmentation of a system that adds additional entities, relations, or interpretations.

Extension Stability.
A property of a structure such that its core identity conditions remain invariant under admissible extensions.

Framework Independence.
A property of a representation indicating that it remains valid under multiple interpretive frameworks.

Collapse.
The situation in which two distinct categories cannot be maintained as separate without contradiction or identity instability.

Structural Constraint.
A restriction on permissible representations imposed by the architecture of the system itself.

Neutral Representation.
A representation that does not encode interpretive commitments beyond the structural substrate.
